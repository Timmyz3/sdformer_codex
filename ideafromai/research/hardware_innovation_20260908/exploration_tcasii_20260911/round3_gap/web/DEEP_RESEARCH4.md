# Research result

**Status: Partial**

None of the verified primary sources implements dual last-use of a post-absorb binary GeMM plus a separate continuous residual/PED tensor, live full-domain BN over \(10\times C\times H\times W\) as a completion barrier, or typed last-use at an AT-LIF \(\{0,\theta\}\) absorb cut. Official AT-LIF emits \(o\in\{0,\theta\}\) and, with fixed inference thresholds, absorbs that scale into the next layer’s weights so the spike path stays binary. FireFly-T last-uses a post-SN 1-bit AND-PopCount attention GeMM together with a continuous pre-neuron membrane residual while still loading \(\theta\) as a live parameter; the other inspected accelerators use ordinary LIF/IF, all-spike IAND residuals, CIM stationarity, AC-only 3-D arrays, or event-OF pipelines without those three objects.

### A. Complete remaining priors

**FireFly-T (arXiv 2505.12771).** The overlay reuses one datapath: an orchestrator, then a sparse engine for 1-bit-activation conv/linear on vectorized spikes, then an optional binary engine—a 2-D systolic array of AND-PopCount PEs—for 1-bit \(QK^\top\) and \(QK^\top V\). Layers pipeline orchestrator → sparse engine → binary engine when attention is on; the sparse engine can form \(Q,K,V\) while the binary engine computes attention; outputs are reformatted to \((P_{T_s},P_{F_x},P_{C_i})\). [S1] Residuals are pre-neuron membrane-potential adds so every convolution keeps spike inputs; the residual tensor is continuous membrane, not a post-neuron spike add. Binary attention plus that pre-neuron residual is the preferred pairing (SDT/SDTv2, BESTformer); SSA is binary matrix multiplication plus a separate conv, and the MLP is spiking neurons with \(1\times1\) convs. [S2] Four 128-bit S-AXI-HP ports load weights, biases, and membrane thresholds; fetch feature-map inputs; write feature-map outputs; and read/write residual data (In FMap, Weight, In/Out Res, Out FMap). Neuronal dynamics sit in the sparse engine, distinct from the binary engine. [S3] Sparse last-use decodes 1-bit activation bitmaps, gathers multi-bit (4-bit) weights at non-zero spike indices, accumulates into membrane with group-wise output-channel updates, then runs a LIF/neuronal-dynamics grid (\(P_{F_x}\times P_{T_s}\)) that still uses the loaded thresholds. [S4] Attention last-use is post-SN binary GeMM: \(Q=\mathrm{SN}(XW_Q)\), \(K=\mathrm{SN}(XW_K)\), \(V=\mathrm{SN}(XW_V)\), then \(\mathrm{SN}((QK^\top/\sqrt{d})V)\) (or thresholding with learnable \(\Delta\)) on 1-bit operands in the AND-PopCount array—not an AT-LIF \(\{0,\theta\}\) payload multiply. [S5] That is dual last-use of a post-SN binary GeMM/attention path plus a separate continuous membrane residual with \(\theta\) still a parameter stream; it is not typed last-use at an AT-LIF \(\{0,\theta\}\) cut with \(\theta\) absorbed into the next \(W\). [S6]

**Spike-IAND-Former (arXiv 2503.19643).** The paper does not name or implement typed last-use at an AT-LIF absorb cut, dual last-use of post-absorb binary GeMM plus a continuous residual/PED tensor, or live full-domain BN over \(10\times C\times H\times W\) as a completion barrier. [S7] The 111/101/000 encoding is three MUXes that unroll LIF across \(T=4/2/1\) and route membrane to neighbor neurons so four timesteps can compute at once—not a last-use protocol at an absorb cut. [S8] Neurons are ordinary LIF (threshold 0.5, leak 0.25); the work does not use AT-LIF or fold a threshold into weights. [S9] IAND replaces residual addition (\(x\cdot(1-\mathrm{ConvBN}(x))\), both operands spikes, multiply reduced to AND) so the residual result stays binary spike I/O, not a continuous residual/PED tensor dual-consumed with a post-absorb binary GeMM. [S10] Membrane SRAM is removed by unrolling the LIF time loop so membrane is not stored, not by last-use retirement of a live tensor. [S11] BN appears only as ConvBN inside IAND; there is no live full-domain BN over \(10\times C\times H\times W\) and no BN hardware completion barrier. [S12]

**Aliyev et al., DATE 2025 (arXiv 2411.15409).** The architecture is a dense MAC core for the non-binary direct-coded input layer and sparse event-driven cores for later binary-spike conv/FC layers (raw float into the first conv, then a thresholding neuron emits binary spikes). Sparse cores compress spike trains and accumulate weights into membrane BRAM. That is not dual last-use of a post-absorb binary GeMM plus a separate continuous residual/PED tensor. [S13] The neuron is standard LIF, \(s_j[t]\in\{0,1\}\), \(u_j[t+1]=\beta u_j[t]+\sum w_{ij}s_i[t]-s_j[t]\cdot\theta\), with subtract-on-fire of \(\theta\) in the Activ unit; it is not AT-LIF \(\{0,\theta\}\) with \(\theta\) absorbed into the next \(W\), and not typed last-use at that cut. [S14]

**ASNA-Flow (TVLSI 2025).** The IEEE abstract states an event-driven asynchronous neuromorphic co-design for event-based optical flow with (1) hardware-aware algorithm optimization, (2) data-pattern analysis, and (3) exploitation of optical-flow spatial locality for sparse computing, in TSMC 28 nm at 104 FPS, 7.9 mW, and 0.3 pJ/SOP. That abstract does not state dual last-use of post-absorb binary GeMM plus a continuous residual/PED tensor, live full-domain BN over \(10\times C\times H\times W\), or typed last-use at an absorb cut. [S22] Identity: *IEEE Trans. VLSI Syst.* 33(12):3409–3422 (Dec. 2025), published 26 Aug 2025, DOI 10.1109/TVLSI.2025.3600953, IEEE Xplore 11142472, Wang, Luo, Li, Zhou, Yu, Xiao (Sun Yat-sen University). The IEEE/Crossref full-text PDF was not retrieved (paywall; no author preprint). [S23]

**FlexSpIM (arXiv 2410.23082 / ISCAS 2025).** Unified CIM SRAM holds weights and membrane potentials so each layer can choose weight-stationary vs output-stationary mapping from its weight vs membrane footprint. That is operand stationarity, not typed last-use of post-absorb binary GeMM and a continuous residual/PED tensor. [S15] Compute is binary IF XNOR-and-accumulate into stored membrane, then compare to a threshold for a spike. “Residual” appears only as a comparison-table label of other chips (Residual CNN / ResNet-12), not as a dual-consumer residual/PED tensor. [S16]

**Fang CICC 2024 and JSSC 2024 follow-up.** The inspected CICC/HKUST abstract is a spiking-only AC accelerator (heterogeneous SNN+CNN MAC arrays rejected) with timestep-parallel 3-D reuse, unstructured non-zero spike–weight fetch, and a multi-operator scheduler; it does not state dual last-use or an AT-LIF \(\theta\)-absorb-into-next-\(W\) cut. [S17] The JSSC paper (IEEE JSSC 60(3):977–989, DOI 10.1109/JSSC.2024.3507095), in the inspected IEEE abstract and lab highlights, lists a 3-D timestep-parallel AC array, a parallel non-zero spike/weight fetcher, and a multimode scheduler for SCONV, Q/K/V, and SSA (plus inter-core router, QKV transpose compressor, SSA zero-skip). Those texts do not describe typed last-use at an AT-LIF absorb cut or dual last-use of binary GeMM plus a separate continuous PED tensor. [S18]

**SENECA FireNet (arXiv 2407.20421).** A FireNet of six convolutions plus two rnn-conv blocks (32-channel \(3\times3\), last layer \(1\times1\)) maps onto eight cascaded SENECA cores. The SNN path adds the weight vector into the LIF membrane (no multiply); the ANN path multiplies BFloat16 activations; rnn-conv Tensor Sum adds two same-block conv streams. That is not a post-absorb binary GeMM plus a separate continuous residual/PED tensor. [S19] Last-use is spatial \(3\times3\) receptive-field completeness, not typed last-use at an absorb cut: after the last RF event, ANN neurons fire and their state is freed (three rows kept); LIF membranes persist to the next timestep and cannot be released. [S20] There is no live full-domain BN over \(10\times C\times H\times W\) as a hardware completion barrier: event-driven depth-first convolution pipelines across layers without per-timestep barrier primitives, one forward pass per event frame; deployed tensors are \(56\times56\) or \(120\times120\) with 32 channels, and “normalization” appears only as a generic remark that ANN-style normalization layers hurt sparsity. [S21]

**Not in the verified packet.** ERAFT (ISCAS 2025), SpikePool (arXiv 2510.12102), and EventShiftFlow (arXiv 2605.28312) have no verified primary-source claims here, so no complete prior is copied and no implementation verdict is drawn.

### Official AT-LIF absorb cut

AT-LIF emits a two-level current \(o=\theta\cdot H(m-\theta)\in\{0,\theta\}\). At inference with fixed thresholds that scaling is absorbed into the subsequent layer’s weights and remains binary rather than graded. [S24] None of the copied priors above is that absorb cut, a typed last-use at it, or dual last-use of the resulting binary GeMM with a separate continuous residual/PED tensor.
