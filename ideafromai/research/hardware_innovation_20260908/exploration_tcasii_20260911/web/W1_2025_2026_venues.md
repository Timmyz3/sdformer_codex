## How CONTROL vs candidate X is assigned here

- **CONTROL:** mechanism is complete enough that a TCAS-II reviewer can say “this is a reskin of that paper,” **or** it is the mandatory relative prior for binary-spike / tick-batch / product-sparsity / dual-engine SNN-Transformer hardware. Fit to dual-consumer θg T10 is usually **no**, because they assume 0/1 spikes and a single accumulate consumer.
- **Candidate X:** a complete mechanism worth copying, but it does **not** already close continuous θg × two consumers (gate + residual/PED) × T10 constant-source compile × same-port service. The increment would have to be measured on this net, not claimed from their numbers.
- **Out of identity:** analog CIM as the contribution; binary-ATLIF identity change; OpenROAD/Yosys as foundry PPA. Recorded, not proposed as the letter.

## Query 1 — HPCA / MICRO / ISCA: Gustavson, product sparsity, tick-batch

### P1. GustavSNN — `NOT_LOCAL` — **CONTROL (dataflow) / weak X only if generalized off binary spikes**

- **Title:** GustavSNN: Unleashing the Power of Gustavson's Algorithm on SNN Acceleration with Column-Parallel Tick-Batch Dataflow  
- **Venue / year:** HPCA 2026 (31 Jan–4 Feb 2026)  
- **DOI:** [10.1109/HPCA68181.2026.11408587](https://doi.org/10.1109/HPCA68181.2026.11408587)  
- **arXiv:** none located. Primary: [IEEE CSDL](https://www.computer.org/csdl/proceedings-article/hpca/2026/11408587/2eA8iOmm784), [HPCA program](https://2026.hpca-conf.org/details/hpca-2026-main-conference/83/GustavSNN-Unleashing-the-Power-of-Gustavson-s-Algorithm-on-SNN-Acceleration-with-Col), [IEEE Xplore 11408587](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11408587/).  
- **Mechanism (5 lines):**
  1. Treats SNN layer as Gustavson (row-wise) sparse product of a spike matrix and weights.
  2. Prior tick-batch SNN accelerators are neuron-centric and cannot exploit temporal sparsity well.
  3. Column-parallel tick-batch (CPTB) partitions the spike matrix and processes each timestep submatrix in parallel while keeping tick-batch semantics.
  4. Neuron states update in local registers (no global membrane-potential memory).
  5. Non-zero row vector (NRV) spike format skips inactive spike rows. Claimed up to 11.8× vs naïve GP and 1.43–1.92× vs SOTA SNN accelerators (abstract variants differ on the SOTA number).
- **Binary spikes?** Yes. Spike matrix + NRV skip of inactive rows.
- **Fit dual-consumer θg T10?** CPTB / local-Vmem / NRV is a strong **tick-batch control**. It does **not** feed two consumers from a continuous θg source: residual/PED still need the amplitude, not a 0/1 row skip. T10 is a dense-ish constant temporal operator, not Gustavson SpGEMM of binary spikes.
- **Verdict:** CONTROL for tick-batch + Gustavson scheduling. Not X unless someone redefines NRV over quantized θg **and** still serves PED.

### P2. Prosperity — `IN_LOCAL` (2503.03379) — **CONTROL**

- **Title:** Prosperity: Accelerating Spiking Neural Networks via Product Sparsity  
- **Venue / year:** HPCA 2025  
- **DOI:** [10.1109/HPCA61900.2025.00066](https://doi.org/10.1109/HPCA61900.2025.00066)  
- **arXiv:** [2503.03379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03379) (verified abs 2026-09-11)  
- **Mechanism:** Product sparsity reuses identical inner products of binary spike rows; Prosperity identifies and eliminates those redundant products at runtime vs bit sparsity (SpikeBERT density 13.19% → 1.23%).  
- **Binary spikes?** Yes, explicitly bit-sparse 0/1 activations.  
- **Fit dual-consumer θg T10?** No as a drop-in. Continuous θg destroys exact 0/1 row equality that product sparsity searches. Dual consumers also need the **value**, not only a reused accumulate.  
- **Verdict:** CONTROL. Do not retitle product sparsity as the letter.

### P3. Bishop — `IN_LOCAL` (2505.12281) — **CONTROL (spiking-transformer ASIC)**

- **Title:** Bishop: Sparsified Bundling Spiking Transformers on Heterogeneous Cores with Error-Constrained Pruning  
- **Venue / year:** ISCA 2025  
- **DOI:** [10.1145/3695053.3731063](https://doi.org/10.1145/3695053.3731063)  
- **arXiv:** [2505.12281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12281) (verified abs)  
- **Mechanism:** Token-Time Bundles; dense vs sparse cores via a stratifier; BSA training for structured TTB sparsity; Error-Constrained TTB Pruning on Q/K/V; attention core is AND+accumulate of binary spikes. 28 nm, 2.96 mm², vs PTB.  
- **Binary spikes?** Yes. Attention is binary AND/ACC.  
- **Fit dual-consumer θg T10?** Heterogeneous dense/sparse cores **look** like dual consumers but they split **density**, not gate-vs-continuous-residual. ECP is lossy attention pruning, forbidden as identity for frozen ATLIF.  
- **Verdict:** CONTROL for spiking-transformer heterogeneous cores. Not X for continuous θg.

### P4. Phi — `NOT_LOCAL` — **CONTROL (pattern reuse)**

- **Title:** Phi: Leveraging Pattern-based Hierarchical Sparsity for High-Efficiency Spiking Neural Networks  
- **Venue / year:** ISCA 2025  
- **arXiv:** [2505.10909](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10909) (verified abs; comments: ISCA 2025)  
- **DOI:** IEEE ISCA 2025 record (arXiv is the open primary).  
- **Mechanism:**
  1. Observes that binary spike vectors cluster into a small set of patterns.
  2. Level-1: vector-wise sparsity — represent activations by pre-defined patterns, **offline precompute** pattern×weight.
  3. Level-2: residual element-wise sparse complement matrix.
  4. k-means pattern selection + pattern-aware fine-tune.
  5. Dedicated hardware processes both levels on the fly. 3.45× / 4.93× vs SOTA SNN accelerators.
- **Binary spikes?** Yes (0-1 activation sparsity).  
- **Fit dual-consumer θg T10?** Level-1 offline pattern×W is a **constant compile** cousin of T10 adder graphs, but patterns are binary spike vectors, not continuous θg, and there is one accumulate consumer. Fine-tune changes the frozen student.  
- **Verdict:** CONTROL for binary pattern precompute. Weak X only if T10 lifting is shown **not** to be Phi Level-1 on a different alphabet — must argue the alphabet is continuous θg + two consumers.

## Cross-cutting verdict for dual-consumer continuous-θg T10

**No 2025–2026 paper found that already implements:** ATLIF continuous threshold amplitude **and** two live consumers (gate + residual/PED) **and** a compiled T10 source **and** event-camera dense optical flow.

What the year **does** contain:

1. **Binary spiking-transformer hardware is now crowded.** ESTU (TCAS-II), FireFly-T, SpikeTA, Bishop, VESTA, ISCAS tick-batch IAND ASIC, 2501.07825 FPGA, Xpikeformer/ASTER analog. A TCAS-II letter that is “spiking transformer accelerator” without the continuous-θg dual-consumer mechanism will be read as ESTU/FireFly-T/SpikeTA.
2. **Tick-batch / Gustavson / product sparsity / dual-side sparsity are CONTROL.** GustavSNN, Prosperity, Phi, Comperity, FireFly-S, LoAS/APEX. All assume 0/1 spikes and one accumulate consumer.
3. **The only mechanisms that look like incomplete A for this net:**
   - **da4ml / ROM-LTE:** compile a constant T10 (or lifting) into adders/LUTs. X = share that cone across **two** consumers under same-port ready, without binary-spike skip.
   - **DATE 2025 hybrid + NeuroFlex:** two datapaths because the tensor is not uniformly spike-sparse. X = simultaneous, not muxed, consumption of **θg-valued** source by gate and PED.
   - **Comperity DSE / Phi Level-1:** share a compiled base, stream a residual — but residual is **bits**, not θg, and there is no second consumer.
4. **ISCAS IAND (P34) is the anti-prior:** they deleted residual add to stay binary. This letter’s identity is that residual/PED **cannot** be deleted.
5. **Event-flow FPGA (ERAFT/ROFD/EventShiftFlow) and SDformerFlow** cover the **task** side. They do not close hardware X.

**Suggested CONTROL stack for a 5-page letter (do not skip):** ESTU (same journal), FireFly-T (dual-engine name), Bishop (ISCA spikeformer), Prosperity/Phi/Comperity (reuse of binary products), GustavSNN (tick-batch), da4ml (if T10 compile is claimed), DATE 2025 hybrid (if two datapaths are claimed), SDformerFlow (if “spikeformer OF” is claimed).

**Suggested X tests (not claims):** same-port service of one compiled T10 cone into (i) integer gate and (ii) q24 PED, with continuous θg, AEE ≤ 1.259 and ΔAEE ≤ +0.005 vs ordinary, net service ≥ 15%. None of the papers above reports that measurement.
