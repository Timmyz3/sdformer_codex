# Research result

**Status: Partial**

Among 2024–2026 HPCA, ISCA, MICRO, ISSCC, CICC, and TCAS mechanisms, Prosperity Product Sparsity, GustavSNN column-parallel tick-batch with NRV, LoAS fully temporal-parallel inner-product spMspM, FireFly-S dual-side Bitmap skip, ConvFormer LFS layer fusion, Phi’s two-level pattern hierarchy, C-Transformer HDSC/OSSU/IWGU, Fang’s 3-D timestep-parallel spiking-attention array, and Kao’s subthreshold 8T CIM remain honest complete priors when cited as those designs. CFMP is cascaded feature-map pruning, not layer fusion, so an increment labeled “CFMP layer-fusion” misnames LFS and renames CFMP. A true increment X is none of those accelerators: it is continuous-amplitude ATLIF θg that is not folded into weights, dual-consumed as a spike-gate and as a continuous residual/PED path, plus native projection BN on live full-domain batch statistics with a phase barrier—identities that AT-LIF, unmasked PSN, membrane-shortcut Transformers, and tdBN only partially overlap.

### Complete 2024–2026 hardware priors

Product Sparsity reuses inner products of common binary 1-subsets of spike-activation rows in spiking GeMM with shared weights (not weight sparsity), issuing suffix rows after prefixes in a one-prefix ProSparsity Forest with tile \(m=256\), \(k=16\), \(n=128\); on SpikeBERT, product density is 1.23% versus 13.19% bit-sparsity, with average 7.4×/1.8× speedup and 8.0×/193× energy versus PTB/A100. [S1] A ProSparsity Processing Unit detects subsets in TCAM, prunes to that forest, and accumulates only XOR residual spikes while bypassing zeros in the ProSparsity pattern. [S13]

GustavSNN partitions multi-bit \(A\) (\(M\times D\)) times 1-bit spike tensor \(B\) (\(D\times N\times T\)) along spike columns: each PE holds a \(P\)-wide membrane-potential register, updates it with in-situ Gustavson products, skips all-zero NRV rows, and finishes all timesteps of a layer before the next, reporting up to 11.8× GOPS/W versus a naïve GP accelerator, 1.43× versus SOTA SNN accelerators, and 11× versus direct GP SpMM. [S2]

LoAS is fully temporal-parallel inner-product spMspM on sparse unary spikes \(A\in\{0,1\}^{M\times K\times T}\) and sparse integer weights \(B\in\mathbb{Z}^{K\times N}\), with \(t\) spatially unrolled innermost so each Temporal Parallel PE accumulates all timesteps of one output neuron from timestep-packed non-silent fibers, a bitmask inner-join, and parallel LIF. [S3] Inner-join uses fast and laggy prefix-sum circuits with a pseudo-accumulator plus per-timestep correction, reporting up to 8.51× speedup and 3.68× energy reduction versus sequential SparTen/GoSPA/Gamma-SNN ports. [S15]

FireFly-S detects dual-side sparsity as a Bitmap AND of a spike vector and a weight mask so only matching nonzero pairs update IF/LIF neurons on a fully unfolded spatial FPGA with inter-layer pipelining and a spatial-temporal dataflow that nests channels inside pixels and the temporal loop inside output channels (membrane-potential storage-free). Training reports >85% weight sparsity (headline 85–95%) with 4-bit quantization; measured energy-normalized throughput is 10,047 / 3,683 / 2,327 FPS/W on MNIST, DVS-Gesture, and CIFAR-10. [S4]

On the ISSCC 2025 ConvFormer chip, layer fusion is the LF Scheduler: LFS reuses on-chip KV for all VA tiles, then replaces KV with convolution weights and fuses those weights with each VA output tile and LA input tile (VACF/LACF; non-overlapped fused convolutions at VA/LA boundaries), cutting overall EMA 86.8–96.2% and, on a 64K-token sub-block with 50% VA ratio, 3.91× EMA and 1.45× energy; HAPU+LFS+CFMP together report 4.66–7.71× speedup and 4.39–7.10× energy versus the paper’s baseline, with 0.22 μJ/token on SegFormer-B0 at 28 nm. [S5] CFMP is cascaded feature-map pruning: each Seg. Head convolution splits into \(W_0\) then \(W_1\), intermediate fmap \(Z\) is pruned with a pre-trained tiled mask (FMS tiled-column offsets), and a density-recovery unit restores a dense output; the same two-factor path can substitute \(Q\), \(K^\top\), \(V\) for sparse vanilla attention, reducing 91.10% of Seg. Head computation, improving sparsity 6× versus zero-skipping, and cutting energy 2.03× when pruning both VA and convolution. [S6]

Phi is a two-level SNN (including spiking-transformer) hierarchy: Level-1 vector patterns with offline Pattern-Weight Products and Level-2 \(\{1,-1\}\) element corrections, generated on-the-fly by a pattern matcher and processed by dedicated L1/L2 units (Preprocessor matcher, L1 PWP retrieval, L2 packed adder tree), reporting 3.45× speedup and 4.93× energy versus Stellar on Spikformer, SDT, and SpikeBERT. [S14] C-Transformer is a Samsung 28 nm homogeneous DNN-Transformer/Spiking-Transformer LLM processor (0.7–1.1 V, 200 MHz) with an HDSC hybrid multiply/accumulate unit, an output-spike speculation unit, and implicit weight generation with extended sign compression, reporting 0.21×–0.33× computation energy and 0.37×–0.41× EMA energy versus baseline, and 2.6–18.1 μJ/token. [S16] Fang et al. fabricate a 40 nm unstructured-sparsity-aware spiking attention/convolution processor with a 3-D compute array that parallelizes timesteps for weight reuse, a parallel non-zero spike fetcher, and a multimode scheduler for SCONV, QKV generation, and spiking self-attention, at 0.078 pJ/SOP and 77.6% ImageNet with a spiking transformer. [S17] Kao et al. use subthreshold 8T SRAM current-mode MAC with in-situ current sensors and distributed regulators, replica-SRAM programmable current firing thresholds, and stride-tick batching that accumulates all timesteps of one feature block on-neuron before striding, cutting equivalent membrane storage from 1488 Kb to 0.375 Kb, and measuring up to 1181.42 TOPS/W at 93.64% GSCD accuracy. [S18]

### Partial overlaps that do not complete X

AT-LIF’s current-based output is \(o_i[t]=\theta_i\cdot H(m_i[t]-\theta_i)=\theta_i s_i[t]\) with \(o_i[t]\in\{0,\theta_i\}\)—a scaled binary, two-level signal, not analog per-event amplitude. [S7] Layer-shared \(\theta\) is absorbed into the next-layer weights at inference, so the deployed model uses binary spikes rather than kept continuous \(\theta_g\) on Conv/PED. [S19]

PSN mixes non-iteratively \(H=WX\) with \(W\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times T}\) so each \(H[t]\) uses all timesteps, including future inputs, then emits binary \(S=\Theta(H-B)\in\{0,1\}^{T\times N}\); future weights must be masked for causal step-by-step inference. [S8] That \(T\times T\) noncausal mix is only a partial overlap: consumers remain binary spikes, and original spike notation cannot replace continuous amplitude \(z=\theta_g\). [S20] \(T=10\) is an experimental setting, not PSN’s defining identity: Fang et al. use \(T=4\) for CIFAR-10/ImageNet PSN and report sliding PSN on CIFAR10-DVS at \(T=4,8,10\), while SDformerFlow-v2 uses PSN with 10 time steps. [S9]

Spike-driven Transformer places residuals on continuous membrane/feature tensors before the spike neuron so transmitted spikes stay binary (\(U_0=u+\mathrm{RPE}\), \(S_0=\mathrm{SN}(U_0)\), \(U'_l=\mathrm{SDSA}(S_{l-1})+U_{l-1}\)); Spikingformer uses \(O_l=\mathrm{ConvBN}(\mathrm{SN}(O_{l-1}))+O_{l-1}\) so ConvBN sees spikes after SN. [S10] The membrane-shortcut keeps the spike matrix binary and rejects SEW integer addition; it does not add a second continuous \(1\times1\) PED consumer of the same residual merge. [S21] QKFormer SPEDS (and SDformerFlow SPE, which cites it) uses a deformed downsampling shortcut \(Y=F(X,\{W_i\})+\mathrm{SN}(W_d X)\) or pre-activation \(\mathrm{SN}(G(X)+W_d X)\), with \(W_d\) a \(1\times1\) stride\(>1\) conv; SDformerFlow writes \(z=\mathrm{BN}(\mathrm{Conv}(\mathrm{SN}(I)))+\mathrm{Conv_{deformed}}(I)\). These are residual identity-mapping blocks, not a named dual consumer of one continuous state as spike-gate versus residual/PED. [S11]

tdBN normalizes channel-wise over the full \(T\times N\times H\times W\) domain with affine \(\lambda,\beta\) shared across time and pre-activations scaled by \(\alpha V_{\mathrm{th}}\); inference uses dataset moving-average \(\mu_{\mathrm{inf}},\sigma_{\mathrm{inf}}\) fused into conv weights, whereas BNTT decouples BN along time-steps with time-wise parameters. [S12] Training therefore uses joint full-domain mini-batch moments, but inference fusion is not live full-domain batch moments as a completion barrier. [S22] Native projection BN is specified as live statistics over shape \([10,96,120,160]\) with a phase barrier that cannot complete before the entire actual BN domain; captured means/variances are verification data only and do not close a hardware endpoint. [S23]

### True increment X

X is not a rename of Product Sparsity, CPTB/NRV, FTP, dual-side Bitmap skip, or CFMP. Completing the TCAS-II identity requires \(\tau\neq\theta\) with continuous \(\theta_g\) consumed after residual merge both as \(\mathrm{proj.sn}\rightarrow\theta_g\rightarrow 96\times96\times3\times3\) stride-2 convolution (spike-gate) and as continuous PED \(U_{\mathrm{ped}}\rightarrow V_{\mathrm{ped}}\) \(1\times1\), while projection BN waits on live \([10,96,120,160]\) batch moments rather than frozen moving-average fusion. [S19]
