\begin{abstract}
Low firing activity does not automatically accelerate event-driven optical
flow: repeated arithmetic and weight delivery remain constrained by finite
storage, ports, and accumulator ownership.  This brief presents two exact
reuse islands for a frozen spiking optical-flow workload.  Both decide reuse
before the resource being suppressed and preserve private completion state.
C1 executes a
Prosperity-compatible exact-subset forest in a single-1RW
store, issues the residual, suppresses unused-parent writes, and retires completion
atomically.  On 51.84 million source rows, its same-ledger cycle model reduces
component time by 40.99\% (1.6945$\times$) versus strongest-zero skipping; a
mapped 28-nm nine-SRAM island occupies 166{,}514~$\mu$m$^2$ and meets 3-ns
setup/hold.  C2 shares one Acc24 fabric across eight typed signed sources.
Token-set broadcast (TSBG) reuses only weight delivery while keeping products,
destinations, and accumulators private.  Across 2,880 fixed real-activity
workloads spanning all 12 FC1 and 12 FC2 layer identities, same-port/cache VCS
reduces post-load cycles by 45.49\% (1.8345$\times$) and scalar weight reads by
58.13\%.  A matched clock-gated implementation adds 0.50\% logic area.
Against equal-bandwidth
K1$\times$8, K8 uses 77.61\%
less logic and provides 4.541$\times$ directed-throughput/logic-area.  Results
remain component-level and prelayout; no whole-network speedup is inferred.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Digital accelerator, event-based vision, optical flow, product sparsity,
single-port SRAM, spiking neural networks.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\IEEEPARstart{E}{vent} cameras make inactivity visible, but sparse arithmetic
is only one term in execution time.  In a binary-event optical-flow
deployment, nonzero sources still trigger parent-state reads, residual
issues, and psum writes.  A design that reports only operation sparsity can
therefore be slower than an equal-resource zero-skipping baseline once the
parent working set is finite and the store is single-ported.

Prosperity~\cite{prosperity} and Phi~\cite{phi} show that useful SNN speedups
come from executable reuse or patterns, not firing rate alone.  Digital
neuromorphic processors such as ODIN~\cite{frenkel2019odin} and
event-stream accelerators such as SpinalFlow and SNE~\cite{spinalflow,sne}
establish spike-driven datapaths.  TCAS-II SRAM compute-in-memory macros
exploit sparsity inside the array~\cite{qiao2022dscim,taylor2022pim}.  Our
focus is the execution circuit for repeated \emph{products} of a
convolution-like layer within a 240-KiB-class total storage budget and a
physical 1RW contract, not a new definition of product sparsity.

This brief addresses two costs that remain after zero-source skipping:
retaining reusable products and repeatedly delivering the same weights.
C1 retains selected parents for the current tile, schedules reads and
writes through one physical port, and suppresses writes without a future
consumer.  C2 shares an Acc24 fabric across eight typed sources; its TSBG
frontend groups consumers before weight reads so that one delivered row
serves several contexts.  Signs, destinations, products, and accumulators
remain private.  The common design principle is to decide reuse before
incurring the access or computation it eliminates.  The frozen Motion
checkpoint supplies the workload; neither circuit requires retraining or
changes to its source descriptors.

\section{Workload and Arithmetic Model}
The frozen Motion C12 ep34 checkpoint obtains 1.199514 average endpoint error
(AEE) on an 825-frame local DSEC validation set and has a 5.6709\% global
firing rate.  Hardware evaluation consumes the sealed ep34 source descriptors
generated by fixed normal inference.  Captured ATLIF sources entering the
evaluated operators are binary; signed source codes represent downstream
polarity and correction traffic rather than analog tensor values.  The frozen
software path uses Motion-XOR scoring with $\alpha{=}0.125$ and K as its value
carrier, while hardware quantization is disabled on that path.  Model, VCS,
and physical evidence remain explicitly tagged and are never multiplied.

Bottleneck convolution exposes repeated products only while parent and
destination state remain resident.  That invariant defines C1.  Attention and
decoder traces remain part of the frozen identity but are not promoted to
performance contributions.

\section{C1 Microarchitecture}
\subsection{Directory and Legal Parents}
C1 first builds a ping-pong directory for each 64-row, 16-source mask task.
For a row with at least two active sources, C1 selects the nonempty
exact-subset parent with maximum population
count, breaking ties by original row index.  Equal-mask parents must have
an earlier original index.  Execution follows stable population-count/index
order.  Both the one-parent forest and this dependency-respecting order are
inherited from the Prosperity artifact~\cite{prosperity}; they are not our
algorithmic contribution.  A static parent-needed bitmap marks rows selected
by at least one child.  A row with no selected child is never written to
parent scratch.  This is unused-result write suppression, not dynamic
last-consumer reclamation.

The 240-KiB-class budget covers the modeled working set, not parent scratch
alone.  Nine $128\times128$ 1RW SRAM leaves operate in
parallel as one $128\times1152$ parent-product scratch and retain 96 signed
12-bit product lanes.  Masks and the live-parent directory are control state;
the downstream psum store is a separate boundary.  The current 64-row task
addresses only 64 entries: selected parents are retained until task completion,
without within-task eviction or allocation-pressure misses.  The engine never assumes
the ideal simultaneous 1R1W access that produced a higher but unphysicalized
reuse ceiling.  The circuit contribution is implementing this established
reuse relation with single-port service, write suppression, and atomic
completion; the measured ratio against zero skipping also includes the
inherited product-sparsity benefit.

\subsection{Residual Issue and Exact Reconstruct}
A selected parent mask $\mathcal{M}_p\subset\mathcal{M}_r$ yields a disjoint
residual.  C1 issues only that residual and reconstructs the 96-lane signed
product row by adding the stored parent:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{P}_r=\mathbf{P}_p+
\sum_{s\in\mathcal{M}_r\setminus\mathcal{M}_p}\mathbf{p}_s.
\label{eq:c1exact}
\end{equation}
Exactness follows from the disjoint union, not from value prediction.  If no
legal nonempty parent exists during directory construction, C1 selects the empty parent and issues the
original source row.  Sign, destination, and accumulator identity are never
coalesced across rows.  Thus sparsity changes delivered work without changing
the operator result.

\subsection{Single-Port Arbiter and Atomic Commit}
The execution side combines one earliest-parent lookahead, a two-entry
reserved response queue, and forwarding under a deadline-aware 1RW arbiter.
A parent-scratch read and a retained-result write compete for the same
macro port; residual arithmetic can overlap a read, and architectural psum
commit is a separate interface.  The arbiter grants the access that would otherwise miss its
row-completion deadline; the reserved queue absorbs one already-accepted
response so that a later grant cannot orphan it.  Forwarding supplies a
just-completed parent to a waiting residual without a macro read.

Final psum write and row completion are a single atomic event.  Backpressure
may change service order, but it cannot expose a partially reconstructed row:
the typed-source terminal bit and the completion pulse retire together.  A
directed VCS tile on the first 64 real support masks of the cycle ledger is
used later only to calibrate these event counts, not to replace the
same-ledger cycle model.

Exactness depends on the subset relation and producer-before-consumer
ordering.  The written bitmap distinguishes scratch-resident results from
not-yet-written producers; forwarding is an alternative source of a valid
parent.  Protocol checks do not implement eviction recovery.  Alternative
parents, dynamic reclamation, and lifetime-minimizing row schedules are not
part of this mapped island.

\section{C2 Typed-K8 and Context-Safe Broadcast}
The C2 fabric accepts up to eight nonzero typed sources and shares the Acc24
array, endpoint, and control rather than replicating eight K1 engines.  Its
fair equal-service baseline is therefore K1$\times$8, not one K1 lane.  Eight
independent memory-bank channels may return out of order; epoch, slot,
generation, tag, slice, and source-channel fields bind every response before it
can enter the signed bridge.  A stale or misrouted response is rejected rather
than accumulated.

TSBG changes only weight delivery.  Four token contexts expose a common
source-group row, so one fetched row can serve four independent signed products
and four Acc24 contexts.  For shared row $W_g$,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{fetch}(W_g)=1,\qquad
 A_c\leftarrow A_c+v_cW_g,\quad(v_c,d_c,A_c)\;\text{private}.
\label{eq:tsbg}
\end{equation}
It is incorrect to share the product because equal source indices can carry
different signs or destinations.  Both ordinary and broadcast modes elaborate
the same 192-bit row-live bitmap, four-row cache, public memory ports, and
hierarchical priority encoder; only the static scheduling order changes from
token-major to source-group-major.  A cache hit is resolved before the bank
request state, so a reused row suppresses the memory request and all downstream
response traffic instead of merely gating the multiplier after a read.

Exact fallbacks are explicit.  If token contexts disagree on a weight-row
identity, C2 emits separate fetches.  If C1 finds no legal nonempty parent
when building its directory, it issues the original source row.  Backpressure may
alter service order but not ownership: terminal and atomic commit retire only
after the corresponding private accumulator is complete.

Figure~\ref{fig:timing} makes the saved circuit event explicit.  Ordinary
token-major execution resolves the same row separately for each context.
TSBG first proves a common row identity, then asserts one SRAM request and
fans out the returned row.  The other three read-enables, addresses, and
responses are never generated; this is memory-traffic suppression, not a
multiplier gate applied after the access.  Product formation and retirement
remain four private operations.

\section{Evaluation}
All physical results use a commercial 28-nm flow at 3.0~ns.  Reported points
are prelayout, use ideal clocks and ZeroWireload, and have no extracted
parasitics.  C1 area includes nine generated SRAM leaves; C2 comparisons are
logic-only and therefore remain separately labeled in Table~\ref{tab:main}.

Table~\ref{tab:c1ladder} exposes the baseline ladder instead of attributing
all inactivity to product capture.  The ledger is 51.84 million source rows
from ten \texttt{zurich\_city\_09\_a} ep34 samples.  Per-sample ratios span
1.661--1.723$\times$, with a 1.6946$\times$ geometric mean.  Strongest-zero
skipping still walks parent-state traffic for every nonzero source;
same-coordinate bit skipping on this ledger removes almost none of that
traffic (1.003$\times$).  Exact parent reuse is what creates the material
reduction.  The remaining 12.25\% gap from C1 to the concurrent-access
ceiling is the tax of the physical single-port contract.  We do not promote
the ceiling as an attainable RTL result.

The cycle model charges one 1RW grant per accepted macro access, residual
issue, forwarding, deadline holds, and atomic commits.  Its cycles are
modeled service events, not CPU runtime or a full-ledger RTL replay.

The mapped nine-SRAM island occupies 166{,}514~$\mu$m$^2$.  Mapped synthesis
and prelayout PrimeTime use the foundry 28-nm standard-cell library and the
nine compiler SRAM abstracts; no Yosys or educational library is used as an
ASIC PPA proxy.  PrimeTime reports setup/hold slack of $+27.9$/$+1.8$~ps at
3~ns under ideal clocks.  Formality passes 16{,}549 mapped-to-mapped compare
points on that netlist pair.  In a separately bounded 64-row, 253-cycle
directed window the island dissipates 29.08~mW and 22.07~nJ.  That energy is
a mixed-corner, prelayout PrimeTime PX estimate without SPEF and without a
frame-level activity factor; it is not energy per DSEC frame and is not a
chip measurement.

A separately sealed ep34 tile uses the first 64 real support masks of the
cycle ledger.  VCS matches 196 accepted issues, 58 parent edges, 31
dead-write elisions, 54/33 macro reads/writes, four forwards, six deadline
holds, 14 stalls, and 64 commits/completions.  Lane values are deterministic
synthetic signed-12-bit data with zero prior psum.  This calibrates modeled
event counts and directed arithmetic; it does not itself produce the
1.6945$\times$ cycle ratio.  A 64-row task is the scheduling quantum, not the
convolution depth; no parent replacement occurs within it.

For numerical binding, eight retained Conv3x3/ConvTranspose weights use
per-output-channel narrow-range INT8 with a power-of-two scale.  The largest
absolute-code bound is 200{,}219, below Acc24; 1{,}280 Python-integer probes
match and the observed final range is $[-29{,}680,27{,}619]$.  On 825 local
DSEC frames the hardware-order mixed-precision candidate obtains 1.197367 AEE
versus a 1.199514 historical baseline, passing the predefined $+0.02$ gate.
Different GPU backend flags make this a compatibility check, not a causal
accuracy improvement.

TSBG is evaluated on 2,880 fixed ep34 workloads selected independently of
performance: 40 samples from four DSEC sequences, all 12 FC1 and 12 FC2 layer
identities, and first/middle/last aligned B4 token quartets.  The G48 engine
handles all FC1 and four FC2 identities directly; the remaining six G96 and
two G192 FC2 identities execute as functionally exact 48-source continuations.  Activity
comes from the frozen checkpoint, whereas arithmetic uses
deterministic directed INT8 verification weights rather than captured model
weights.  Naturally nonzero descriptors in this population carry $+1$;
directed negative-source tests separately cover the signed protocol.  The
G48 population covers 1,920 workloads and the continuation batch covers 960.
Both use identical public ports and cache capacity;
Table~\ref{tab:main} reports post-load execution.  The G48
subset spans 2.381--2.546$\times$ by sequence.  The continuation population
contains 554 improvements, 404 ties, and two regressions of one and seven
cycles (worst 0.99987$\times$); these tails remain in the ratio of sums.

The shared verification memory uses bank-dependent one-to-nine-cycle
responses, periodic request backpressure, and independent issue/commit
stalls.  These service assumptions are identical across modes, not measured
foundry-SRAM latency.  Consequently, the ratios characterize this frontend
and its response-assembly contract; they do not establish superiority over
an arbitrary fully overlapped streaming architecture.  A faster memory or
another refill/compute overlap policy requires a new matched comparison,
rather than multiplying a separate streaming gain into Table~\ref{tab:tsbgseq}.

\emph{Pre-read causality.} A separate directed VCS ablation compares ordinary
token-major execution, group-major post-read gating, and group-major pre-read

receives, and identity-checks the redundant weights before discarding them.

completes 4,608 signed products and 24 commits with zero mismatch.  Thus the
75\% request reduction in this directed case occurs before SRAM access;
reordering followed by post-read gating does not produce it.  This isolates
request causality, not matched area or energy, and is separate from the
2,880-workload result.

\section{Related Work}
Prosperity already discovers subset/prefix parents, issues residual
activations, and dependency-schedules their reconstruction~\cite{prosperity};
C1 does not reclaim those ideas.  Its circuits claim is their execution under
a tile-resident parent-product store and one physical 1RW port, including
deadline arbitration, reserved response, forwarding, unused-parent write
suppression, and atomic completion.  C1 inherits stable population-count
ordering; it does not actively shorten parent lifetime.  Phi stores
hierarchical patterns chosen by DSE~\cite{phi}.  We adopt their evaluation
pattern---cycle simulation plus RTL/physical anchors and a baseline
ladder---without relabeling their numbers as ours.

FireFly-T already broadcasts wider vectorized data from one memory bank across
spatial-temporal work~\cite{fireflyt}; ELSA, SpikeX, and Bishop establish bundled
delivery and spatial/temporal weight reuse~\cite{elsa,spikex,bishop}.  TSBG does
not reclaim broadcast, bundling, group-major order, or weight reuse.  Its
specialization decides a row hit before the SRAM request and shares only that
delivery among typed signed contexts while destination, tag, terminal,
product, and Acc24 ownership remain private under the same cache/port contract.
This direct prior also precludes comparing K8 against a single K1 lane.

SpinalFlow and SNE establish event-driven execution and state
locality~\cite{spinalflow,sne}; ODIN demonstrates a silicon neuromorphic
core~\cite{frenkel2019odin}.  Their PPA is not an interchangeable baseline
for these digital reuse islands.  TCAS-II WS-LOS combines weight/local-output
reuse with skipping~\cite{wslos2023}, while delta weight sharing reduces
traffic through static inter-network representation~\cite{an2024dws}.
Our weights are unchanged; the evaluated event is a runtime read shared by
private contexts.  Request counts, logic cost, and memory energy therefore
remain separate metrics.

A 28-nm optical-flow chip separates operations, memory traffic, latency,
energy, and AEE~\cite{ciccflow}.  We likewise separate same-workload component
comparisons, public-artifact opportunities, and published chip results,
without ranking prelayout estimates against silicon energy.

\section{Scope and Limitations}
C1 reuses products under a 1RW service constraint; C2/TSBG reuses weight
delivery while retaining private accumulation.  Their ratios are not
multiplied, and neither proves a whole-network latency improvement.

In the 2,880-workload direct-VCS population, TSBG has seven slightly slower
nonempty cases because group-major order can
lose ordinary-cache residency under weak reuse.  These cases remain in the
ratio of sums.  The reported population spans every FC1/FC2 layer identity but
uses three fixed B4 regions per layer/sample; it does not imply full-FC wall
time.  Ordinary scheduling remains an exact fallback.

C1's mapped nine-SRAM island and full 214,912-B ledger are separate evidence;
the 105-macro capacity is a 0.988-mm$^2$ area model rather than integrated PPA.
Its 22.07-nJ result is a directed prelayout window, not frame energy.  C2/TSBG
has prelayout gated setup/hold repair and a three-window logic-energy
comparison; post-CTS timing robustness and SRAM-inclusive power remain open.
Its common 288-KiB weight store is modeled separately.  No component ratio is promoted to
monolithic FPS, energy/frame, or whole-network speedup.

\section{Conclusion}
Exact reuse requires matching data lifetime to physical service.  C1 combines
finite parent residency with single-port reconstruction; C2/TSBG combines
shared weight delivery with private signed accumulation.  Their evaluation
separates modeled product-capture latency, RTL-observed read suppression, and
28-nm circuit cost.  The resulting component design targets redundant
transfers that remain after ordinary zero-source skipping, without changing
the evaluated source arithmetic.
